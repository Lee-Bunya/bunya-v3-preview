The short answer

What is paraplanning?

Paraplanning is the technical work behind personal financial advice: checking the client’s information, researching strategies and products, running the modelling and drafting the Statement of Advice for the adviser to approve. Firms do it in-house, use contract or outsourced paraplanners, or have AI prepare a draft that a person checks. The adviser stays responsible for the advice.

Key takeaways
  • A paraplanner prepares the advice documents; the adviser decides the strategy and stays responsible for the advice.
  • If you outsource paraplanning, ASIC is clear that licensees keep their obligations, including over client data.
  • Measure time from strategy meeting to approved SOA, not to first draft, and count the query rounds.

What does paraplanning cover?

It’s Thursday afternoon. Mara Holloway has just finished a strategy meeting with Priya and Arjun Shah, who are planning to retire at 60 and 63. The advice is clear in her head. Now someone has to turn it into a Statement of Advice: check the fact find, run the numbers, research the options and write it up in the firm’s template. That work is paraplanning.

In most firms, paraplanning covers:

  • checking the fact find for gaps, conflicts and out-of-date figures
  • researching strategies, products, platforms and insurance
  • modelling: cash flow, retirement and super scenarios
  • drafting the SOA and the documents that go with it
  • preparing implementation paperwork once the client signs
  • preparing for reviews

What paraplanning doesn’t cover is the advice decision. ASIC says individuals who give personal advice to retail clients must comply with the best interests duty and related obligations, and must give the client an SOA, a document that helps them understand, and decide whether to rely on, the advice. A paraplanner prepares the work. The adviser decides the strategy, approves the SOA and stays responsible for it.

In-house, outsourced or AI-assisted paraplanning: what’s the difference?

There are four common ways to resource paraplanning, and many firms mix them. Contract paraplanning usually means an independent paraplanner working on your files under contract. Outsourced paraplanning services usually means a business that takes files from many firms.

What should you check before outsourcing paraplanning?

ASIC’s review of offshore outsourcing by advice licensees found paraplanning and administration were the main services outsourced. It found most licensees reviewed didn’t have adequate arrangements for assessing, appointing and monitoring those services. ASIC is plain that licensees keep ultimate responsibility under the Corporations Act. As ASIC Commissioner Alan Kirkland put it, licensees ‘cannot outsource their fundamental obligations’.

Before you appoint a contract paraplanner or an outsourced service, check:

  • what your licensee requires before you appoint a provider, and whether it needs to approve them
  • who will see client data, from where, and on what devices
  • how files are sent and stored, and how data is returned or deleted when the work ends
  • that the contract covers use, access, retention and disposal of data, which ASIC expects
  • that Australian privacy laws will be met, and the provider follows your cyber security requirements
  • how you’ll monitor quality and turnaround over time, not just at the start

How do you brief a paraplanner well?

Most rework starts with the brief. A paraplanner can’t read the adviser’s mind, and every gap becomes a query, a guess or a delay. Use this template for every file:

  1. Client and scope. Who the advice is for, what it covers and what it doesn’t.
  2. The fact find. The date it was confirmed with the client, and any known gaps.
  3. Goals, in the client’s words. From the approved meeting note, not from memory.
  4. The strategy. What you’re recommending, the alternatives you considered and why you ruled them out.
  5. Products and platforms. Your preferences, and your licensee’s approved product list if it has one.
  6. Modelling assumptions. Set by the adviser, written down.
  7. Fees. From your fee schedule, including any ongoing fee arrangement.
  8. Anything else. Insurance, estate planning referrals, aged care, special circumstances.
  9. Deadline and contact. When it’s needed, and who answers questions.

The ‘why’ in point 4 saves the most time. A paraplanner who knows why an option was ruled out won’t write it back in. A fact find that shows where each answer came from and modelling with the assumptions recorded make points 2 and 6 much quicker to brief.

What quality checks should a paraplanning process include?

A clean-looking document isn’t evidence that anything was checked. Build checks in at each stage:

  • Paraplanner self-check. Every figure traced to its source, every template section complete, open questions listed rather than assumed.
  • Technical check. A second person for complex strategies, such as contributions near caps or structures the firm rarely recommends.
  • Adviser review. Recommendations match the strategy, the reasons are specific to this client, the fees are right.
  • Licensee review. Where your licensee requires pre-vetting, build its turnaround into the timeline.
  • After the fact. Sample completed files each quarter, as you would for an audit-ready advice file.

Track what each check finds. If the adviser keeps correcting the same thing, fix the brief or the template, not just the draft.

How should turnaround and handover work?

Agree the stages and who holds the file at each one: brief accepted, queries raised, draft returned, adviser review, changes, approved. Then measure the whole trip, from the strategy meeting to the approved SOA. A fast first draft followed by three rounds of queries isn’t fast.

A few habits make handover smoother:

  • keep queries and answers in one place on the client record, not spread across inboxes
  • name versions clearly, and only ever have one person editing
  • send the draft back with a short handover note: assumptions used, open items and anything that changed from the brief
  • set a time for queries to be answered, so files don’t sit waiting on the adviser

What does a well-run paraplanning handover look like?

Worked example (fictional)

Aynub Financial is a fee-for-service practice in Kingaroy, Queensland. After the Shahs’ strategy meeting, Mara Holloway completes the firm’s brief template: retirement and super advice only, the fact find confirmed with Priya and Arjun the week before, their goals from the approved meeting note, the strategy and the two options she ruled out, with reasons.

An AI draft of the SOA is prepared in Aynub’s template from those inputs, and goes to the firm’s contract paraplanner to refine. She notices that Arjun’s super balance comes from a statement that’s now months old. Rather than carrying it forward, she raises a query on the client record. The office requests the latest statement, she updates the figure and notes the change in her handover.

Mara reviews the draft against her strategy, tightens one of the reasons and approves it. The firm records how long the file took from strategy meeting to approved SOA, and how many query rounds it needed.

Where does AI drafting change paraplanning, and where doesn’t it?

AI changes the assembly. Given a confirmed fact find, the modelling and the adviser’s strategy, it can draft the SOA in your template, place figures from their sources and keep the structure consistent. That moves the paraplanner’s time from typing towards checking, refining and technical work.

It doesn’t change the judgement. AI can’t decide the strategy, judge whether advice suits the client, or notice what the client didn’t say. It shouldn’t fill a gap with a plausible guess. A draft is a draft until an authorised person has checked it, and the adviser still approves what goes to the client. Your next step: take your last five SOAs and write down, for each, the days from strategy meeting to approved SOA, and where the time went: waiting for a brief, waiting on queries, drafting or rework. That tells you whether you need more paraplanning capacity, a better brief or faster drafting.

Common questions

Is a paraplanner the same as a financial adviser?

No. A paraplanner prepares the research, modelling and advice documents, and the adviser gives the advice. ASIC says individuals who give personal advice to retail clients must comply with the best interests duty and related obligations.

What’s the difference between contract and outsourced paraplanning?

The terms overlap. Contract paraplanning usually means an independent paraplanner working on your files, while outsourced paraplanning services usually means a business handling files for many firms. Either way, check data handling and quality monitoring before you start.

Does offshore paraplanning raise extra issues?

ASIC’s review found paraplanning was one of the main services advice licensees outsource offshore, and that oversight was often weak. ASIC expects careful selection, ongoing monitoring, contract terms on data, secure transmission and compliance with Australian privacy laws.

What should a paraplanner do when the fact find has a gap?

Raise it as a query for the adviser and record it on the file. A gap filled with an assumption is hard to spot later and can flow straight into the advice.

Will AI replace paraplanners?

AI can draft, but it can’t decide the strategy or judge suitability. In practice the paraplanner’s work shifts from writing towards checking, refining and technical analysis.

How do you measure paraplanning quality?

Track how many drafts the adviser approves with only minor changes, how many query rounds each file needs, and what reviews and audits find. Fix recurring issues in the brief or the template.
