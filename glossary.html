A

AFCA

The Australian Financial Complaints Authority, the free, independent external dispute resolution scheme for financial services complaints. AFS licensees that serve retail clients must be members.

Source: AFCA: About AFCA

AFS licence (AFSL)

An Australian financial services licence, issued by ASIC. To give financial product advice, a business must hold one or be authorised by a business that does.

Annual renewal consent

For ongoing fee arrangements entered into from 10 January 2025, the client’s signed, dated written consent to continue, given each year after a written disclosure of the coming period’s services and fees. It must fall in a window from 60 days before to 150 days after the arrangement’s anniversary or agreed reference day.

Fee-for-service renewalsReview PacksSource: ASIC INFO 286: Ongoing fee arrangements and consents

Authorised representative

A person or company authorised by an AFS licensee to provide financial services on its behalf. The licensee remains responsible for the services they provide.

Source: ASIC: Authorised representatives

B

Best interests duty

The duty in section 961B of the Corporations Act to act in the client’s best interests in relation to personal advice given to a retail client. It doesn’t apply to general advice.

Best interests duty and the safe harbour stepsSource: ASIC RG 175: Financial product advisers, conduct and disclosure

C

Code of Ethics

The Financial Planners and Advisers Code of Ethics 2019. Relevant providers must comply with it, alongside their other professional standards.

Source: ASIC: Professional standards for financial advisers

Conflicted remuneration

A benefit that could reasonably be expected to influence the financial products an adviser recommends, or the advice they give, to a retail client. It’s banned under the Corporations Act, with limited exceptions.

Source: ASIC RG 246: Conflicted and other banned remuneration

Continuing professional development (CPD)

The ongoing training relevant providers must complete each year to stay authorised: at least 40 hours a year, across categories ASIC sets out.

Source: ASIC: Continuing professional development (CPD)

CSLR

The Compensation Scheme of Last Resort. It can pay eligible consumers when a financial firm hasn’t paid compensation awarded by AFCA, and it’s funded by levies on financial firms, including advice licensees.

Source: ASIC: FAQs on the Compensation Scheme of Last Resort

D

DBFO

Delivering Better Financial Outcomes, the government’s reform package responding to the Quality of Advice Review. Its first stage, the DBFO Act 2024, rewrote the ongoing fee rules and removed the fee disclosure statement.

Fee disclosure statementsSource: ASIC: Delivering Better Financial Outcomes package

Design and distribution obligations (DDO)

Rules requiring product issuers to design products for a defined target market and distribute them accordingly. Advisers count as distributors, but personal advice providers are exempt from the reasonable steps obligation; they still report to issuers and keep records.

Source: ASIC INFO 264: Design and distribution obligations for advice licensees and advisers

F

Fact find

The record of a client’s relevant circumstances, objectives, financial situation and needs that personal advice is based on. Showing where each answer came from makes advice easier to check.

Fact findSource: ASIC RG 175: Financial product advisers, conduct and disclosure

Fee disclosure statement (FDS)

The yearly statement advisers used to give clients in an ongoing fee arrangement, showing fees paid and services received. The DBFO Act removed the requirement, replacing it with annual renewal consent.

Is the FDS still required?Source: ASIC: Delivering Better Financial Outcomes package

File note

A record of a client meeting or call: the client’s circumstances, what was discussed, what was agreed and what happens next, checked by the adviser. A transcript records what was said; a file note records what matters for the advice.

Financial Advisers Register

ASIC’s public register of relevant providers. Licensees must notify ASIC when a person becomes a relevant provider.

Source: ASIC: Professional standards for financial advisers

Financial Services Guide (FSG)

A document, or website disclosure information, that tells retail clients about the services offered, how the provider is paid and how to complain.

Source: ASIC INFO 291: FSGs and website disclosure information

G

General advice

Financial product advice that isn’t personal advice: it doesn’t take account of the person’s objectives, financial situation or needs.

Source: ASIC RG 175: Financial product advisers, conduct and disclosure

General advice warning

The warning given with general advice to a retail client that it doesn’t take account of their objectives, financial situation or needs.

Source: ASIC: Obligations when giving financial advice

L

Licensee

The holder of an AFS licence. Advisers either work under a licensee or their firm holds its own licence (self-licensed). Licensees often approve the systems their advisers use.

O

Ongoing fee arrangement (OFA)

An arrangement where a retail client who receives personal advice pays a fee over a period of more than 12 months. From 10 January 2025 it continues only with the client’s annual renewal consent.

Fee-for-service renewalsSource: ASIC INFO 286: Ongoing fee arrangements and consents

P

Paraplanner

The person who prepares the advice behind the scenes: research, modelling and drafting the Statement of Advice for the adviser to review and approve.

Paraplanning

Personal advice

Financial product advice where the adviser has considered one or more of the client’s objectives, financial situation or needs, or a reasonable person might expect them to have. It brings the best interests duty and a Statement of Advice.

Statement of Advice guideSource: ASIC RG 175: Financial product advisers, conduct and disclosure

Product Disclosure Statement (PDS)

The document a product issuer gives retail clients setting out a financial product’s features, fees, risks and benefits, so they can compare products.

Source: Corporations Act 2001 (Federal Register of Legislation)

Q

Quality of Advice Review (QAR)

Treasury’s independent review of the advice rules, with its final report in December 2022. It found SOAs too long and complex and led to the DBFO reforms.

Source: Treasury: Quality of Advice Review, Final Report (December 2022)

R

Record of Advice (ROA)

A shorter record that confirms advice, used instead of a Statement of Advice where an exemption applies, such as further advice when the client’s circumstances haven’t significantly changed.

Statement of Advice guideSource: ASIC INFO 266: Records of Advice

Relevant provider

An individual authorised to give personal advice to retail clients on relevant financial products. They must meet the professional standards and be on the Financial Advisers Register.

Source: ASIC: Professional standards for financial advisers

Reportable situations

Breaches and other situations that AFS licensees must report to ASIC, in most cases within 30 calendar days of first having reasonable grounds to believe one has arisen.

Advice file review checklistSource: ASIC: Reportable situations for AFS and credit licensees

Risk profile

An assessment of a client’s attitude to and capacity for investment risk, usually recorded in the fact find and reflected in the advice. It isn’t a term defined in the Corporations Act.

Fact find

S

Safe harbour steps

The seven steps in section 961B(2) of the Corporations Act. Proving each one is one way, though not the only way, to show the best interests duty was met.

Best interests duty and the safe harbour stepsSource: ASIC RG 175: Financial product advisers, conduct and disclosure

Scaled advice

Personal advice limited to particular topics. ASIC says all personal advice can be scaled; the adviser still has to ask the questions needed to understand the client’s relevant circumstances.

Best interests dutySource: ASIC INFO 267: Tips for giving limited advice

Statement of Advice (SOA)

The document a retail client receives with personal advice, to help them understand the advice and decide whether to rely on it. It must be clear, concise and effective, and is generally given before any further service connected with the advice.

Statement of Advice guideSOA generatorSource: ASIC: Statement of Advice

T

Target market determination (TMD)

The document a product issuer makes describing the class of consumers a product is likely to suit. ASIC expects advisers to consider it as part of meeting the best interests duty.

Source: ASIC RG 274: Product design and distribution obligations
