AI meeting notes for financial advisers: from conversation to checked file note

The short answer

How should financial advisers use AI meeting notes?

AI meeting notes for financial advisers are a draft, not the record. Settle consent and recording before the meeting, let the tool draft the file note, actions and follow-up email, then have the adviser check names, figures and decisions. Only the approved note is filed to the client, and every action gets an owner and a due date.

Key takeaways
  • Settle consent, recording and storage before the first client meeting is captured, not after.
  • Check four things in every draft: who said it, the figures, what was decided and who owns each action.
  • Judge a tool on the time to an approved note and assigned tasks, not on how fast the first draft appears.

What do AI meeting notes for financial advisers actually do?

It’s 4.40 on a Thursday. You’ve finished your third review of the day, the next client is in the waiting room, and the file note from this morning’s meeting is still a page of scribbles. By Monday, the detail that mattered, the client mentioning a possible redundancy, is a vague memory.

AI meeting notes for financial advisers are built for that gap. With the client’s agreement, the meeting is recorded and transcribed, and the tool drafts a structured file note, a list of proposed actions and often a follow-up email. Good tools link each point back to the moment it was said, so the adviser can check it quickly.

What they don’t do is decide what the record says. A fluent summary can still drop a qualification, confuse two speakers or turn a maybe into a decision. The adviser’s review is what turns a draft into the firm’s record.

What does a good AI meeting-note workflow look like?

Five steps, in this order. Skipping the first or the fourth is where most problems start.

  1. Prepare. Confirm the approved tool, who is attending and how the client will be told about recording. Have an alternative ready if the client would rather not be recorded.
  2. Capture. Tell the client at the start. Note any interruptions, unclear audio or off-record discussion, so the reviewer knows the limits of the source.
  3. Draft. Separate what the client said, what was discussed, what was decided and what is still open. Anything missing stays marked as unconfirmed rather than filled in.
  4. Check and approve. The adviser checks the draft against the source and their own recollection, corrects it, and approves it. Record who approved it and when.
  5. Assign and follow up. Approved actions become tasks with a named owner and a due date, linked to the client record so the whole team can see them.

The same workflow carries into the rest of the advice process. The approved note feeds the fact find and, later, the advice document: see our guide to AI statement of advice drafting.

Is a transcript the same as a file note?

No. Each output does a different job, and each needs a different kind of check.

OutputWhat it is forWhat to check
RecordingThe original source.Consent, storage location and who can access it.
TranscriptA text version of what was said.Speaker labels, names, figures and gaps in the audio.
AI draft noteA condensed first version of the meeting.Omissions, lost qualifications and conclusions nobody reached.
Approved file noteThe firm’s checked record.Accuracy, context, decisions and who approved it.
Action listThe agreed follow-up work.Right client, named owner, due date and status.

What should an adviser’s meeting file note include?

Use this as a starting structure and adapt it to your licensee’s requirements and the type of meeting. It isn’t a prescribed compliance template.

  • Meeting details: client, date, meeting type, attendees and their roles.
  • Source: how the meeting was captured, how consent was given, and any gaps in the recording.
  • Purpose: why the meeting happened and what it covered.
  • Client updates: changed circumstances, goals and information supplied, attributed to the person who said it.
  • Discussion: topics, questions, options raised and any qualifications.
  • Decisions: what was agreed, by whom, and what is still undecided.
  • Actions: each task, its owner, its due date and anything it depends on.
  • Review: who checked the note, when, and what they corrected.
Keep what was said apart from what it means

“Arjun said he might cut back to three days” is a fact. “Arjun is retiring next year” is an interpretation. Only the first belongs in the note unless the client actually confirmed the second.

What does checking an AI draft look like in practice?

Worked example (fictional)

Mara Holloway, an adviser at Aynub Financial, a fee-for-service practice in Kingaroy, meets Priya and Arjun Shah for their annual review. They’re planning to retire at 60 and 63. Arjun mentions his employer is restructuring and he might drop to part-time next year. Priya says she’ll send her latest super statement. No change to the strategy is agreed.

The AI draft reads: “Arjun retiring early. Adviser to update retirement strategy.” Mara checks the linked moment in the recording and corrects both points. The approved note says Arjun may move to part-time work, nothing is decided, and Priya’s statement is outstanding.

Two tasks come out of it: the client services officer requests Priya’s statement within a week, and Mara reviews whether new modelling is needed once it arrives. The suggested strategy change never became an instruction, because nobody approved it.

Do you need client consent to record and transcribe a meeting?

Treat consent as something to settle before you capture anything. The OAIC’s guidance on commercially available AI products includes a worked example on using AI to take meeting minutes. It says the meeting’s personal information must be reasonably necessary for your functions (APP 3), and that whether participants are told about the recording and transcript, and whether they consent, is relevant to whether collection is lawful and fair.

The same guidance recommends, as best practice, that organisations don’t enter personal information, and particularly sensitive information, into publicly available generative AI tools. Client meetings are full of both, so a free consumer note-taker is the wrong tool.

Before rollout, have the right people confirm the recording laws that apply to you, your privacy obligations and your licensee’s policy. Then ask any supplier where audio, transcripts and notes are stored, who can access them, whether your data is used to train models, and how retention and deletion work.

ASIC’s Report 798, Beware the gap, reviewed how 23 AFS and credit licensees use AI and govern it. It’s useful context for your own AI policy. It doesn’t endorse any product.

How do you test an AI meeting-notes tool before you commit?

Run a short pilot with sample or authorised meetings, and score the whole workflow, including the time it takes to correct and file the output.

  • Accuracy: names, dollar amounts, negatives (“we won’t sell the unit”) and two people talking at once.
  • Traceability: can the reviewer jump from a point in the note to the moment it was said?
  • Format: does the draft follow your file-note structure without inventing missing answers?
  • Approval: is the draft kept separate from the approved record, with the reviewer named?
  • Client matching: does the note land on the right client, and can a wrong match be fixed?
  • Follow-up: do approved actions become real tasks, or does someone retype them?
  • Data: storage location, access, retention, deletion and export.
  • Total effort: correction time and missed actions, not just drafting speed.

A practical next step for this week: pull your last five file notes and time how long each took to write and file. That’s the baseline any tool has to beat.

Common questions

Can AI write a financial adviser’s file notes?

It can draft them from an approved recording or transcript. The adviser still checks accuracy, context and actions before the draft becomes the firm’s record, and the approval should be recorded.

Is a meeting transcript enough as a client file note?

Usually not. A transcript records what was said but doesn’t clearly separate decisions, open questions and next steps. Use your licensee’s record-keeping requirements to decide what an approved file note must contain.

Is a Teams meeting invite enough to count as consent to record?

Don’t assume it is. Tell the client at the start of the meeting, offer an alternative if they’d rather not be recorded, and have your own consent process checked against the laws and licensee policy that apply to you.

What if the AI gets a figure or instruction wrong?

Correct it before approval and check whether the error has already reached a task or email. If the recording is unclear, ask the client rather than filling the gap with a guess.

Can AI meeting notes update our client records automatically?

Some tools write straight to the client record, which is risky without a review step. Look for a workflow where only the approved note is filed and only accepted actions become tasks.

Do AI meeting notes work for meetings outside the office?

Only if the tool can capture them. Online meeting tools cover video meetings and calls; meetings at a client’s home or business need a separate recorder that captures consent and files the note the same way.
