The short answer

How do you choose financial planning software in Australia?

Choosing financial planning software in Australia starts with the type, not the brand: an all-in-one planning platform, a practice CRM, an AI note tool, an AI agent added to your current platform, a system built on Microsoft 365, or spreadsheets. Then test your shortlist on one real household’s full journey, and compare ownership, approvals and cost.

Key takeaways
  • Compare options of the same type first. A note tool and a whole practice platform can’t be judged on the same demo.
  • Test every shortlisted option on one household’s full journey, not on a feature tour.
  • Ask where your data lives, who approves what, and what you keep if you leave, before you ask about price.

Why does the type of software matter more than the brand?

Three demos in a fortnight. The first showed a beautiful meeting summary. The second showed a planning platform with a strategy library you’d never finish exploring. The third showed a CRM with neat pipelines and dashboards. Each one looked like the answer, and none of them could be compared with the others.

That’s the most common trap when choosing financial planning software in Australia. The tools on offer don’t do the same job. Some write up meetings. Some run the advice process from fact find to SOA. Some hold the client record and leave the advice work elsewhere. Some sit on top of the system you already have.

So start one step earlier. Decide which type of software fits the problem your firm actually has, then compare products within that type. You’ll see fewer demos, and the ones you see will answer your real question.

Most options in the Australian market fall into six types. Many firms run two or three of them side by side.

Which type of software suits which firm?

Match the type to the problem you can name today, not the one a demo suggests you have.

  • Writing file notes is the bottleneck, and the rest of your process runs well: an AI note tool may be enough. Read AI notetaker or practice system before you decide.
  • Work stalls between steps: actions get lost, reviews slip, fee consents run late. The gap is the workflow, so look at a practice system that holds the whole journey.
  • Your team types the same details into several systems: look for one client record that the fact find, advice and review all draw from.
  • You’re happy with your platform but want AI help: an agent on top may suit, as long as you accept the platform’s limits and costs stay.
  • You want records and documents in your own Microsoft 365: compare the Microsoft-based options on who designed the advice workflow and who maintains it.

What matters most once the demo is over?

When advisers talk about switching, or about why they wish they had, the same themes come up again and again. They’re worth more weight in your decision than any single feature.

  • Typing it once. Details entered in the fact find should flow to the advice, the review and the client’s record without anyone retyping them.
  • Follow-through. A meeting should end with the note, the actions, the client email and the next step all moving, not just a summary in an inbox.
  • Approval on the record. AI can prepare work, but a person should approve it, and you should be able to show who approved what, and when.
  • Where your data lives. Know which country, which cloud and whose account holds your clients’ records, and where any AI processing happens.
  • Terms you can live with. Renewal dates, notice periods, price-rise clauses and how you get your data out if you leave.
  • Support your team will actually use. Training for every role, not just the advisers, and a named person to ask in the first weeks.

What should be on your buyer’s scorecard?

Copy these questions into a spreadsheet, one column per option, including staying where you are. Ask every supplier the same questions, in writing.

  1. Can you show one household going from first meeting to implemented advice, live, without switching systems?
  2. Which details does our team type more than once, and where?
  3. What does AI prepare, and what does a person have to approve before anything is used or sent?
  4. Where is each approval recorded, and can we show it to our licensee?
  5. Where are client records, documents and recordings stored, and where does AI processing happen?
  6. How are meetings recorded with the client’s consent, including meetings away from the office?
  7. How is the price counted (per seat, per household, per module or by usage), and what happens when we hire or grow?
  8. Which of our current tools does it replace, and which will we keep paying for?
  9. What are the renewal, notice and price-change terms?
  10. If we leave, what do we keep, in what format, and who helps us move?
  11. What training does each role get, and who supports us after go-live?
  12. When we need something new, can our own team change it or build it, without a developer, and who approves it before it goes live?

How do you run a demo that tells you the truth?

A feature tour shows you what the software can do. A scenario shows you what your team will actually do. Ask every supplier to run the same scenario with example data built on a real household from your firm, with names and identifiers changed.

  • Start at the first meeting and finish at implemented advice, then jump to the annual review.
  • Ask to see the moment a person approves AI-prepared work, and where that approval is kept.
  • Ask what happens to a correction: does it flow back to the client record, or stay in a document?
  • Bring your paraplanner and client services lead. They do the work between the steps, and they’ll spot the retyping.
Keep client data out of demos

Use example or de-identified data in any trial or demo, and check your licensee’s requirements before real client information goes into a new system.

Common questions

What is the best financial planning software in Australia?

There isn’t one best option for every firm. The right choice depends on the problem you’re solving, how many people do the work and how your firm wants to own its data. Decide the type of software first, then compare products of that type on one real household’s journey.

Do small advice firms need a full planning platform?

Not always. A small firm that writes its own SOAs and runs reviews well may only need a good client record and a way to write up meetings. Firms where work stalls between steps usually get more from a system that holds the whole journey.

Can we keep our current platform and add AI?

Yes, many firms do. You keep the system your team knows, but you keep paying for it, and the AI can only work within what that platform allows. Compare that against moving the work to one system.

What should we ask about AI in financial planning software?

Ask what the AI prepares, where it runs, which data it uses and what a person must approve before anything is used or sent. Ask how AI-prepared work is marked and where approvals are recorded.

How long does it take to switch financial planning software?

It depends on how much history moves, the quality of your current data and how many people need training. Ask each supplier for a plan that includes a trial migration, testing on your own work and training for every role.

Should we involve our licensee before choosing?

Yes, if your firm is with a licensee. They may need to approve new systems, especially anything that produces advice documents or uses AI. Ask suppliers what they provide to help with that approval.
